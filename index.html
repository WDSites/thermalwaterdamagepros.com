Water damage restoration in Thermal

What water damage restoration covers

Water damage restoration is the process of removing water, drying the structure, cleaning what was touched and rebuilding what could not be saved. In Thermal, CA, it often starts with a call at night, because a burst pipe, a failed water heater, an air conditioner drain or a storm does not wait for business hours. We run an emergency line around the clock so the first step happens quickly, and the rest of the plan is explained before work starts.

The eastern Coachella Valley is a desert with very hot summers and only a few inches of rain in an average year. That can lull homeowners into thinking water is not a risk here. It is, because leaks from plumbing and cooling systems happen in every climate, and when rain does arrive it can be heavy. In 2023, Tropical Storm Hilary brought local emergencies across the valley and a shelter opened in Thermal.

Thermal sits in Riverside County about 25 miles southeast of Palm Springs and a short drive north of the Salton Sea. Our crews work from ZIP 92274 and cover the neighboring towns of Coachella, Mecca, Indio and La Quinta. Each stage of a loss is handled by the same team, so you are not coordinating separate companies.

Before the crew arrives

If water is still spreading, do this first

A dispatcher walks you through these on the phone. Stick to what is safe, and leave any step that puts you near electricity or dirty water.

  • Stop the source

    Shut off the main water valve or the fixture valve so the water stops adding up.

  • Stay clear of power

    If water has reached outlets, appliances or the panel, do not wade in. Call the utility or 911 first.

  • Lift and photograph

    Raise valuables off wet floors and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

  • Keep people out

    Keep children and pets out of standing water, especially if it came from a drain or outdoors.

  • Skip the vacuum

    A household vacuum and standing water are a bad mix. Leave wet extraction to the crew.

  • Call dispatch

    Give the cross streets, the water source and how deep the water is. The line is open 24/7.

Local knowledge

Water damage in Thermal: what local homeowners should know

Thermal began as a Southern Pacific Railroad camp, and a Union Pacific line still runs through town. State Route 86 and State Route 111 are the main routes, and they carry farm freight toward Interstate 10 in Indio. When you call about a flooded kitchen, the dispatcher asks for your cross streets and the nearest highway so the crew can route straight to the address.

Well-known places in the area include The Thermal Club, a private motorsports facility south of town, and Jacqueline Cochran Regional Airport about 1.6 miles southwest. Desert International Horse Park sits west of Thermal, and Desert Mirage High School and a College of the Desert center serve the area. Landmarks help a crew confirm an address quickly, and we use them when a rural road has no obvious street sign.

Water in this part of the valley comes from more than one source. The Coachella Valley Water District serves much of the eastern valley, and some homes were historically served by small mutual water systems. The name on your water bill tells you which provider to call about a main line problem, and your plumber or your insurer will ask for it.

Insurance claims

Records your insurer will ask for

Documenting a claim

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while a slow leak or neglect often is not. Flood is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage, and a new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase. Check your policy and ask your insurer what they need, since coverage is their decision.

We provide moisture readings, photos, a written scope and a list of what was removed. The California Department of Insurance publishes a residential property claims guide that explains the process in plain terms.
