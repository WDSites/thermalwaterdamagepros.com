Big households, many renters and a lot of daily water use

The 2020 Census counted roughly 1,100 housing units in Oasis, with about 96 percent occupied. Slightly more than half of occupied homes were rented, and the average household had more than four people. Those figures describe how many people share each home's plumbing. They do not tell us how many homes have had leaks.

Heavy daily use can wear on supply lines, drain connections and water heaters, and a small leak is easy to miss when everyone in the house is busy. A damp cabinet, a soft spot near the bathroom or a musty smell is worth a call even when the stain looks small. If you rent, tell the landlord or manager right away and keep dated photos.

If water is still spreading in your Oasis home

These steps fit a house, a rented unit or a manufactured home. Keep them simple and stay safe.

  • Find the shutoff

    Close the fixture valve or the main valve if you can reach it without walking through water.

  • Respect the power

    If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

  • Keep people out

    Keep children and pets away from water that came from a toilet, a drain or the outdoors.

  • Lift what you can

    Move bedding, papers and electronics off wet floors into a dry room.

  • Take dated photos

    Photograph the water and the damage before any cleanup begins.

  • Call the dispatcher

    Say you are in Oasis and describe the source and how deep the water is.

Electrical safety first

Never touch a breaker or plug in a fan while standing in water. If you see sparks or smell gas, leave the home and call 911.
