Reading the signs in a larger space

Water in a big room often shows up far from where it started. A damp corner can be the end of a long path under flooring or inside a wall, so the visible stain is rarely the whole story.

Safety in the first hour

Protect people first, then the building. Staff and customers should leave any room with active dripping or standing water.

  • 1
    Electrical Stay clear of puddles around outlets, panels and equipment. Switch power off only from a dry, reachable panel, and put a call to the utility or 911 ahead of ours if there is any shock risk.
  • 2
    Water source Close the valve that feeds the leak if you can reach it safely. If you cannot find it, tell the property manager right away.
  • 3
    Notify Tell the owner or manager and your insurance contact early, since leases often set notice times.

How a large floor gets dried

We start with an extraction unit to remove standing water, then map the damage with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera. That map shows wet concrete, wet wall bases and wet insulation that the eye misses.

Air movers push dry air across surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. A bigger floor needs more of both, and it needs more monitoring visits, because readings are taken in many spots over several days. Our structural drying and dehumidification work is measured against ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference standard.

Wet drywall or insulation that does not dry is cut out, and surfaces are treated with an antimicrobial product matched to the water type.

Work zones, shared walls and staying open

Some businesses try to keep operating during drying. We can plan work zones so wet areas are contained and equipment sits out of walkways, and we will put the scope in writing. We cannot promise that any space can stay open, because that depends on the water, the equipment and the safety of the room.

In multi-unit buildings, water moves through shared walls, floors and ceilings. A leak in one unit can wet the one beside it, so we check adjoining spaces with the meter and tell the owner or manager what we find.

Owner, tenant and the insurance claim

Who pays usually comes down to two documents, your lease and your policy. Leases often split duties between the owner and the tenant, so read yours for who maintains the pipes, the roof and the interior finishes.

Commercial policies often cover sudden, accidental water and often do not cover slow leaks. The California Department of Insurance notes that commercial policies typically exclude flood, which needs its own policy, and it publishes a residential property claims guide that still helps you understand how claims move.

Mistakes to avoid after a commercial leak

Do not run fans and call it done. Moving air without removing the moisture can push humidity into the next room and leave the walls wet.

Do not cover wet floors with new coverings or paint over stains. Do not throw out damaged goods or finishes before photographing them. And do not wait on the owner's decision while the water keeps spreading.
