An air conditioner makes water every hour it runs. The coil pulls moisture out of the indoor air, and a drain line is meant to carry that water outside. AC leak safety steps for the first hour

Water and electrical equipment sit close together in an AC leak, so slow down before you start mopping.

  • 1
    Turn the system off Set the thermostat to off. A running system keeps making water, and a frozen coil keeps melting.
  • 2
    Stay out of wet electrical areas Do not touch wet equipment or stand in water near an air handler, outlet or cord.
  • 3
    Breaker only if it is safe Work the breaker only when the panel is dry and easy to reach. If you smell gas or see sparks, call the utility or 911 first.
  • 4
    Catch and document Set out buckets, lift furniture and rugs, and take photos before anything is moved.
  • 5
    Book your HVAC contractor The drip will return until the drain, coil or unit is repaired.

How we dry and rebuild after an AC leak

We begin with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera, because AC water rarely stays under the stain. Readings along the wall, the ceiling below and the attic floor show how far it spread. Wet insulation comes out, and drywall that stays wet usually has to be cut out too.

Standing water goes first, using an extraction unit on floors. Then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for several days, with fresh readings each visit. We dry to the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard, and a HEPA air scrubber runs during demolition to keep dust out of the living space.

After that we rebuild what was removed, including insulation, drywall, texture and paint. If you see dark growth or smell a musty odor, a mold follow-up is its own service with its own scope, handled by our mold remediation team. Schedule the HVAC visit before the ceiling is closed so nobody has to reopen it.

How Thermal heat, farms and housing shape AC leaks

Summers here bring many days over 110 degrees F, so a system can run almost nonstop for months. That sends a lot of condensate through a small drain line, and a line that is slimed up in June can be fully blocked by August. A drain flush before the hot season is a small job next to a replaced ceiling.

Canal-irrigated farm fields surround the community. If you live near them, fine dust and plant matter can reach filters and coils, so change filters on a schedule and ask your HVAC contractor to check the line each spring.

Census figures show many single-family homes and apartments in nearby Coachella were built after 2000. If your home is from that period, it may have an attic or closet air handler with a pan under it, so check those spots first. In apartments, a drip upstairs can stain a neighbor's ceiling, and we also serve water damage in La Quinta and nearby Indio where the same steps apply.

Insurance and the sudden versus gradual question

Insurers separate sudden damage from gradual damage. A pan that overflowed in one afternoon is more likely to be treated as sudden. A condensate seep that stained a wall for weeks is often treated as gradual, which many policies exclude.

Coverage depends on your policy, so ask your insurer before assuming either way. For the claims process, the residential property claims guide from the California Department of Insurance (insurance.ca.gov) is the place to start. Dated photos and moisture readings help show when the water started.

Most repeat AC leaks come from the same few habits.
