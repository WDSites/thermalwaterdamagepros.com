Safety first: water, power and generators

Storms bring outages, and outages bring risks that have nothing to do with the water on the floor.

  • 1
    Stay out of standing water If water is around outlets, cords or an appliance, do not walk through it. Switch off power at the panel only when the floor around it is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • 2
    Call the right people For downed lines, gas smell or sparking, call the utility or 911 before you call a restoration line.
  • 3
    Generators go outside Run a generator in open air, well away from doors, windows and the garage. Exhaust is deadly in enclosed space.
  • 4
    Watch for debris Broken glass, nails and wet roofing are common after wind. Wear boots and gloves.
  • 5
    Photograph from dry ground Take photos before you move anything, then call.

What wind-driven rain and runoff leave in a home

Rain does not fall straight down in a storm. It is thrown sideways into door thresholds, sliding door tracks, window corners and attic vents, and it soaks the edges of rooms that never feel the weather on a normal day.

Runoff adds a different problem. It brings silt, mud and plant debris that dry into a fine film on tile, cabinets and the underside of baseboards. Washing that out is as much a part of the job as pulling the water.

Storm cleanup versus flood work

A storm leak is water that came in through the envelope of the house. Floodwater is different, because it rises from outside and may carry sewage, chemicals and soil. Contaminated water needs porous materials removed, and that is flood territory.

If the water in your home is dark, smells of sewage or came up through a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep people out. Otherwise clean rainwater that was caught early can usually be handled as emergency water removal and then dried.

Storm claims and the flood policy gap

The California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners policies typically exclude flood, so a separate flood policy is needed. A new National Flood Insurance Program policy generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, which means buying one after a storm is too late for that storm. Check your policy and ask your insurer how it treats wind-driven rain.

Its residential property claims guide at insurance.ca.gov explains the process. Keep photos, a list of wet items and our readings, and hold on to damaged materials until the adjuster signs off.

Mistakes to avoid after a storm

  • 1
    Waiting for it to dry Walls and carpet pad hold water long after the surface feels fine.
  • 2
    Sweeping silt dry Dried silt becomes fine dust and works into floors.
  • 3
    Using household vacuums A standard vacuum is not made for wet floors and can be a shock risk.
  • 4
    Running a generator indoors Never do this, even in a garage with the door open.
