Water damage repair is the rebuild that follows drying. Walls, ceilings, flooring, trim, cabinets and paint that were damaged or removed get replaced so the home is whole again. Call (760) 769-1159 and the team will scope the rebuild after the structure has dried, because closing a wall over damp framing is how mold problems start.

Repair comes after mitigation and drying and cleanup. It is a different job with a different timeline, and it often involves finishing trades such as drywall hangers, floor installers and painters. We coordinate the rebuild so the repair matches the scope that was documented during drying.

Ceiling and wall water damage repair

Ceiling stains and wall damage are the most common repair calls. A brown ring on a ceiling means water reached the drywall, and a soft patch means the gypsum has started to break down. Painting over the stain hides it for a few weeks, but it does not fix the cause or the wet material beneath it.

A proper repair finds and stops the leak first, then dries the cavity. Damaged drywall is cut out beyond the stain, wet insulation is removed, and new board is hung, taped, textured and primed. Matching the original texture is its own skill, and the last coat of paint should cover the whole wall or ceiling so the patch does not show.

  • 1
    Find the source a roof leak, a bathroom above, an air conditioner condensate line or a supply line each need a different fix.
  • 2
    Dry the cavity framing and insulation are measured with a moisture meter before anything is closed.
  • 3
    Cut and replace drywall is cut back to dry, solid material and replaced rather than patched over wet board.
  • 4
    Finish to match texture, primer and paint are matched so the repair blends in.

Hardwood floors: cupping and when to wait

Hardwood that cups after a leak is telling you moisture is trapped under the boards. The edges rise because the underside is wetter than the top. In many cases the floor can be dried with mats and air movers and the cupping eases over weeks.

Sanding and refinishing too early traps moisture and can make things worse. The right order is dry, measure, wait, then decide between sanding, replacing a few boards or replacing the floor. If a leak happened recently, ask for moisture readings before anyone quotes a repair.

Dry desert air can also shrink boards after they dry, leaving gaps that were not there before. That is normal physics and it is why final repair decisions wait until the floor has settled.

Does repair need a permit or a licensed contractor?

California's Contractors State License Board says work worth $1,000 or more generally needs a licensed contractor, and the threshold was raised from $500 on January 1, 2025. You can check any contractor's license on the CSLB website at cslb.ca.gov. Our crews are licensed and insured restoration professionals.

Permits depend on the work and the address. Unincorporated Thermal falls under Riverside County Building and Safety. Within city limits, such as Coachella, Indio or La Quinta, the city's building department handles it. Ask the building department that covers your address before major structural or electrical work.

What sets the price of a rebuild

A written scope comes first, and scope and price are explained before work starts. These are the factors that decide the total.

  • 1
    Square footage more wall, ceiling or floor area means more material and more labor.
  • 2
    Materials standard drywall, tile and laminate cost less than hardwood, custom cabinets or plaster.
  • 3
    Drying and prior work a structure that was dried and documented properly needs less rework.
  • 4
    Rebuild scope cosmetic touch-ups and full room rebuilds are priced very differently.
  • 5
    Finish matching matching texture, stain or paint on older homes adds steps.

On insurance, sudden and accidental damage is often covered, and slow leaks or poor maintenance often are not. Your insurer decides what is covered, so ask them what documents they need and share the written scope and moisture readings.
