We have no data on how many homes in Thermal sit on concrete slabs, and no data on how often slab leaks happen here. So this page does not assume either one. If your home sits on a concrete slab, it explains what a leaking line under it can look like and what we do about the water left behind.

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs beneath or inside a concrete floor. The water follows cracks, joints and edges, then rises into flooring and the bottom of walls. Signs the water may be coming from under the floor

No single sign proves a slab leak, but several together are worth acting on.

  • 1
    A warm spot on the floor A patch that feels warm underfoot can point to a hot water line leaking below. It is a clue, not proof.
  • 2
    A hissing or trickling sound Listen in a quiet house with every fixture off. A steady sound from the floor or a wall deserves tracing.
  • 3
    A meter that moves with everything off Shut off all fixtures and appliances, then watch the meter. Movement means water is escaping somewhere, though not necessarily under the slab.
  • 4
    A rising water bill A jump with no change in how you use water suggests a hidden loss.
  • 5
    Damp carpet or baseboards at a slab edge Moisture along an outer wall or at a floor edge can mean water is rising from below.

Safety steps at the meter and inside the house

The first job is to stop adding water, and the second is to keep people away from hazards.

  • 1
    Close the main valve Turn the main shutoff fully closed where the line enters the house, or use the valve at the meter. If neither works, call your water provider.
  • 2
    Watch the electrical Stay out of standing water near outlets, cords and appliances. Flip the breaker only when the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Phone the utility or 911 about electrical or gas danger before you phone us.
  • 3
    Lift and photograph Raise furniture and rugs off wet floors and take photos before moving anything.
  • 4
    Call a plumber Water stays off until the line is found and repaired, so book a licensed plumber or leak-detection company early.

How we pull water out from under and around flooring

Water on a slab has nowhere to drain, so it spreads sideways into whatever sits on top. A moisture meter and a thermal camera map the wet area, then an extraction unit removes standing water. Carpet pad, wet baseboards and soaked drywall at the bottom of walls usually have to come out.

Drying follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 reference standard. Air movers push air across and beneath lifted flooring, and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room. We open or lift flooring where needed so air reaches the layer underneath, and we keep taking readings until materials are dry.

Concrete holds moisture and dries slowly. If the water came from a drain line rather than a supply line, we add antimicrobial treatment and remove porous materials it touched.

Water bills, permits and licenses around Thermal

Much of the eastern valley gets its domestic water from the Coachella Valley Water District, while some Thermal addresses sit on small mutual water systems. Whoever sends your bill is the one to phone if the trouble is on the main line. Ask that provider where its responsibility ends and yours begins.

Opening a floor for a pipe repair can involve permits, and the office depends on your address. Riverside County Building and Safety covers permits in unincorporated Thermal and Mecca, but the city of Coachella has its own department. Confirm which office has your address before the floor is opened.

Jobs of $1,000 or more now generally require a licensed contractor, a rule in place since January 1, 2025. A line repair under a slab can easily reach that figure. Check any plumber's license on the California Contractors State License Board website, cslb.ca.gov, before you sign.

Slab leak insurance claims and the sudden versus gradual test

Insurers separate sudden damage from gradual damage. A line that failed and flooded a room in a day looks sudden. A slow leak that ran under the floor for months is often treated as gradual, and many policies exclude that.

Some policies treat the water damage and the cost of reaching the pipe differently, so ask your insurer how yours reads. Your insurer's own rules come first, and insurance.ca.gov, run by the California Department of Insurance, carries a residential property claims guide.

Mistakes to avoid with a suspected slab leak

A few common moves make the damage bigger or the repair harder.

  • 1
    Leaving the main open Every hour of running water adds to the wet area.
  • 2
    Covering wet floors New flooring over a damp subfloor traps moisture.
  • 3
    Pulling flooring blindly Removal without readings can miss wet areas or take out dry material.
