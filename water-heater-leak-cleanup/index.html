A tank that fails or seeps can flood a closet or garage floor quickly. We pump it out and dry the walls and slab around it.

Water heaters fail two ways. Some split open and dump a tank of water in minutes, while others seep from a fitting or the relief valve for weeks before anyone sees a puddle. Either way, call (760) 769-1159, because our line is open 24/7 and we start with water damage cleanup the same day when a crew is free.

Where the heater sits decides the damage. A garage unit wets a slab and the bottom of a wall, while a hall closet puts water into carpet, drywall and the room next door. We do not replace heaters. A licensed plumber does that, and we dry the home around the work.

Permits, heat and who covers your address

A new heater in most homes needs a permit, and the office that issues it depends on where you live. Unincorporated Thermal and Mecca fall under Riverside County Building and Safety, while Coachella, Indio and La Quinta run their own city departments. Ask the building department that covers your address, and have your plumber confirm before the swap.

The plumber's price matters as well. California Contractors State License Board rules generally require a licensed contractor for work worth $1,000 or more, so a heater replacement will usually land in that range. You can check a license at cslb.ca.gov.

Desert heat adds its own pressure. With summer days often past 110 degrees F, a garage or closet heater runs hot and works hard, and wet materials in that heat go musty quickly. Neighbors in La Quinta and Mecca deal with the same climate.

Safety steps before you touch a leaking heater

Heaters combine water, power and sometimes gas, so the order below is not optional.

  • 1
    Gas smell If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911. Never relight a pilot or flip a switch near it.
  • 2
    Cold supply Close the cold water valve above the tank. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • 3
    Power or gas Switch off the heater's breaker, but only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. A gas unit has its own control dial to set to off.
  • 4
    Standing water Stay out of any water that reaches outlets or cords. Call the utility if you are unsure.
  • 5
    Do not drain Leave the tank alone until the plumber arrives, since hot water can scald.

Drying a closet, garage or utility room

A heater leak is usually clean water, so it is a good case for fast action. We pull standing water with an extraction unit, then check the floor, baseboards and lower wall with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera.

Closets are the hard part. Narrow walls trap water, so we often cut a small opening at the base and run air movers and an LGR dehumidifier through it. Water mitigation on a garage slab is simpler, but rust at the edge and wet drywall still need attention.

We name ANSI/IICRC S500 as the industry reference for the drying goals. Carpet pad and wet insulation next to the heater commonly come out, and we tell you which before we cut.

Insurance and claims for a heater leak

Insurers separate sudden damage from gradual damage. A tank that splits tends to be treated as sudden, while a fitting that seeped for weeks may be called gradual and handled differently. Ask your insurer how your policy reads, and do not assume either way.

The California Department of Insurance publishes a residential property claims guide at insurance.ca.gov. Keep the old heater or at least its label, and take photos of rust and stains before cleanup, since they show how long the leak ran.

What changes the cost of heater leak cleanup

Location is the biggest factor. A garage with a bare slab is a smaller job than a carpeted hallway closet. After that comes how long the water ran, how far it spread and whether it reached the neighboring room.

Materials, wall openings and the days of drying equipment all count. Scope and price are laid out for you before any work begins. We keep dollar figures off the phone because we have not seen your heater room yet.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent a repeat

Most second floods come from a missed warning sign. These habits help.

  • 1
    Ignore the drip A small puddle under the tank is the early warning. Call a plumber before it grows.
  • 2
    Block the relief line The discharge pipe must stay open and unobstructed.
  • 3
    Skip the drain pan A pan with a drain line limits damage when a tank fails.
  • 4
    Relight a pilot Never relight after a gas smell. Wait for the utility.
