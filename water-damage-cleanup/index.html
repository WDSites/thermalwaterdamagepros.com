Water damage cleanup is the removal of water-soaked debris, contaminated materials and wet contents, followed by sanitizing and deodorizing the affected area. It sits between stopping the water and rebuilding. Cleanup answers one question: what has to leave the house so the structure can dry clean?

What gets removed and what gets saved

The decision depends on what the material is, what kind of water touched it, and how long it stayed wet. A crew works through it in order instead of tossing everything.

  • 1
    Usually saved solid wood framing, concrete, tile, many hard-surface contents and clean-water-soaked items dried quickly.
  • 2
    Often removed wet insulation, carpet pad, drywall that wicked water above the baseboard and particle board furniture that swelled.
  • 3
    Removed when contaminated porous materials that touched sewage or floodwater, no matter how new they are.
  • 4
    Documented first photos, a written list and moisture readings, so your insurer sees what was removed and why.

Cleaning contents after a leak

Furniture, clothing, books and electronics can often be saved if you act early. Hard items can be wiped, dried and sanitized. Upholstery and mattresses that sat in contaminated water usually cannot be cleaned to a safe standard.

Photos, papers and sentimental items need separate handling. Freezing wet paper can hold it until it can be dried properly, and a crew can advise on what is worth the effort. Electronics that were wet should not be plugged in until a professional has checked them.

Ask for contents to be moved to a clean, dry room before any demolition starts. It keeps dust and debris off things you want to keep and makes the inventory list easier to write.

Sanitizing, odors and the first 48 hours

Once debris is out, surfaces are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial product matched to the water category. Odor is a sign that moisture or contamination is still in the building. Masking it with air freshener does not fix it, and the source has to be found and removed.

Mold can begin to grow on wet organic material within 24 to 48 hours, per EPA guidance, which is why cleanup and drying run side by side. If mold has already appeared, the job moves to the mold remediation team.

When cleanup becomes an emergency

A small clean-water spill is a cleanup job. Overflowing toilets, backed-up drains, storm runoff and long-standing water are emergencies that need a crew tonight. Use the 24/7 line for any of them.

Never use a household vacuum on wet floors or touch wet electronics. If water reached an outlet or appliance, the safe step is to stay out and call the utility or 911 for electrical risk before calling a restoration line.
